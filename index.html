01 / Our purpose

HBCU expertise belongs in the conversation.

We want to make it easier for HBCU researchers and students to find each other, share expertise, and build lasting relationships in HCI.

HBCUs create pathways to educational opportunity and social mobility. UNCF’s research documents their contribution to movement from lower-income households into the middle class and above. This community builds on those strengths—with opportunities to shape research, mentor students, and collaborate across institutions.

Our aim is stronger, sustained HBCU participation in HCI and CHI—as authors, collaborators, mentors, and community leaders. We welcome non-HBCU colleagues who want to build reciprocal partnerships centered on HBCU priorities.

02 / The proposed meet-up

Ninety minutes.
Connections that continue.

In person · Informal and facilitated
Date, time, and room pending acceptance

A welcoming progression from meeting new people to identifying shared interests and choosing next steps. Expand each activity to explore the plan.

0–10 minWelcome & representation board

Meet the organizing team and learn about the session. Add your institution to an optional board to see the HBCUs and partner institutions represented in the room.

10–25 minHCI Connection Bingo

Meet a student researcher, a first-time CHI attendee, or a colleague from another institution. Conversation starters include “What can I share?” and “What would help my next step in HCI?” Seated paired conversations offer an equivalent alternative.

25–50 minThemed small-group conversations

Choose a conversation about mentorship and student pathways, research partnerships, or sharing HCI methods and resources. Faculty and students participate together, capturing useful ideas and potential connections.

50–70 minHBCU–HCI Collaboration Wall

Share notes under “I Can Offer,” “I Am Looking For,” and “Let’s Build.” Explore overlapping interests and meet potential collaborators. No formal project proposal or commitment is expected.

70–85 minCommunity priorities & commitments

Together, identify two or three achievable priorities, invite volunteer contacts, and choose a first follow-up activity. Contribute verbally or in writing.

85–90 minClosing & next steps

Recap the shared priorities and exchange contact information voluntarily. An optional interest form and paper alternative are planned for the session. Joining the continuing network is separate from attending the meet-up.

Is this an accepted CHI 2027 event?

Not yet. This site describes a proposed meet-up. Acceptance and scheduling are not confirmed. Session details will be updated when available.

Do I need to be affiliated with an HBCU?

No. The community centers HBCU priorities and welcomes non-HBCU researchers, practitioners, and students interested in reciprocal mentorship and collaboration.

Do I need a paper or prior HCI experience?

No advance submission, preparation, or CHI publication is needed. If accepted, the in-person meet-up will be open to registered CHI attendees, subject to room capacity.

What if I prefer to listen or stay seated?

Speaking, writing, listening, and passing are all welcome. We plan seated alternatives, readable paper materials, and participation without a smartphone. Contact sharing will be voluntary.
